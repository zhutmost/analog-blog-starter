Dark magic does not cease to exist merely because you refuse to discuss it.

This course introduces the fundamental forms, traces, and defensive principles of dark magic. You will learn to identify threats under pressure, select an appropriate defensive spell, and neutralize a curse before an error produces irreversible consequences.

Be advised: **courage is no substitute for judgment, and waving a wand is no substitute for training.**

> “The greatest danger is rarely the spell itself, but the wizard who remains convinced that he knows what to do while acting out of fear.”

## Course Objectives

Upon completing this course, students should be able to:

- Identify common curses, hexes, jinxes, and forms of mental interference;
- Infer the probable origin of dark magic from its residual traces;
- Cast basic shield charms and counter-spells reliably;
- Maintain an effective defensive stance in confined environments;
- Determine when to counterattack and when to retreat;
- Avoid injuring themselves through recklessness, hesitation, or incorrect pronunciation.

This course will not teach you how to appear heroic. It is concerned only with whether you can remain composed when confronted with an actual threat.

## Course Content

### Part I: Threat Identification

Dark magic commonly leaves observable evidence, including unnatural cold, scorch marks on nearby surfaces, persistent ringing in the ears, or emotional disturbances that cannot be explained by ordinary causes.

Before taking action, you must answer at least three questions:

1. Is the magic still active?
2. Is it bound to a location, an object, or a specific individual?
3. Could an attempted removal trigger a secondary curse?

When you cannot answer these questions, the appropriate response is not to experiment with a counter-spell you vaguely remember from a book. You should **isolate the area and request qualified assistance**.

### Part II: Fundamental Defenses

The course will focus on repeated practice of the following spells:

| Spell               | Primary Purpose                               | Common Error                                                        |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Protego`           | Deflects directly cast offensive magic        | Casting too late or holding the wand at an incorrect angle          |
| `Finite Incantatem` | Terminates certain persistent magical effects | Using it before identifying the nature of the enchantment           |
| `Expelliarmus`      | Disarms an opponent                           | Using an unnecessarily wide motion and exposing a defensive opening |
| `Impedimenta`       | Slows the movement of a target                | Focusing on one target while ignoring surrounding threats           |

You will repeat these spells until the correct movement becomes instinctive. Merely remembering the incantation **does not constitute mastery**.

### Part III: Curses and Counter-Curses

Dispelling a curse is considerably more complicated than raising a shield. Some curses attach themselves to objects, some transfer through physical contact, and others deliberately present a superficial effect that is easy to remove.

When dealing with an unidentified curse, follow this sequence:

1. Isolate the affected object or individual;
2. Record all visible and perceptible magical phenomena;
3. Examine the area for chained or conditional triggers;
4. Select a targeted counter-curse;
5. Continue observation for at least fifteen minutes after removal.

Skipping any of these steps may transform an ordinary classroom incident into a matter for the hospital wing.

## Classroom Requirements

Every student must bring a wand, the assigned textbook, parchment, and sufficient ink for the entire lesson. Protective eyewear will be supplied when exercises involve corrosive or unstable magic.

The following conduct will not be tolerated:

- Casting spells at another student without permission;
- Using offensive magic that has not yet been taught;
- Touching an object marked as dangerous;
- Offering “I merely wanted to see what would happen” as an explanation.

Late arrival will result in a loss of points. Forgetting your wand is neither a valid excuse for lateness nor grounds for exemption from practical work. It is merely evidence that you arrived unprepared.

## Assessment

The final grade consists of the following components:

- **Classroom Exercises: 30%**
  Assessment of casting accuracy, reaction time, and defensive posture.

- **Written Assignments: 20%**
  Analysis of representative dark-magic incidents and suitable methods of containment or removal.

- **Midterm Examination: 20%**
  Threat identification and basic defensive casting under controlled conditions.

- **Final Practical Examination: 30%**
  Independent handling of a simulated incident containing multiple sources of interference.

Marks will not be awarded because you “almost succeeded.” When facing dark magic, almost succeeding generally means failing completely.

## Recommended Reading

In addition to the assigned textbook, students are advised to consult:

- _Common Curses and Their Counter-Curses_
- _Defensive Charms: Theory and Practice_
- _A Guide to the Identification of Dangerous Magical Objects_
- _Distance, Timing, and Judgment in Magical Duelling_

Some of these volumes are kept in the Restricted Section. Written authorization is required before they may be borrowed. Forging a signature will result in a loss of points and an immediate report to your Head of House.

## A Final Reminder

Defense Against the Dark Arts is not a collection of spells for displaying in corridors. Nor is it a stage upon which you may indulge heroic fantasies.

It demands patience, discipline, and the ability to make a correct decision while surrounded by confusion.

Some of you will find this course exceedingly difficult.

That should come as no surprise.
